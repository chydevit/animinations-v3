# Funan (នគរភ្នំ / ហ្វូណន): Khmer-language sources, claims check and glossary

Compiled 2026-10-09 for the Khmer-narrated Funan documentary. Raw texts are in `../source/` (01–15). They are
untrusted web downloads, kept for reference only. Wikipedia text came from the km.wikipedia API
(`prop=extracts&explaintext=1`). The news texts were stripped from HTML.

**Big caveat about Khmer Wikipedia:** the main Funan article (ហ៊្វូណន) and several king articles were written by one
editor who uses an **archaic or idiosyncratic spelling**: ឝ for ស, ឞ for ស, វម៌្ម for វរ្ម័ន, វ្នំ for ភ្នំ, ភ្វូណាន for
ហ្វូណន, កុរុង for ស្ដេច. **Do not copy these spellings into the narration or subtitles.** Use the standard forms in the
glossary (§3). Parts of the article are also clumsy machine translations of English Wikipedia.

### 01 – Funan (main article): claims
- Funan (扶南, Fúnán; Vietnamese Phù Nam) is a Chinese name. Many Khmer authors hold that it transcribes Old Khmer **vnaṃ /
  bnaṃ** (modern ភ្នំ, "mountain"). This is Coedès's thesis. The king's title was "kurung bnaṃ" (Skt. *parvatabhūpāla /
  śailarāja*). The article itself also notes the native name is unknown and that 南 may simply mean "south".
- Funan was centred on the lower Mekong / Mekong delta. Chinese texts put it 3000 li west of Linyi (Champa) on a large
  gulf, with a big river flowing from the west.
- The capital is variously identified:
  - **Vyādhapura**, which Coedès read as "city of the hunter" and Vickery as "trapper" (Chinese Temu 特牧 → Old Khmer
    *dmāk*). It was placed at Ba Phnom (Prey Veng) by Coedès and Pang Khat.
  - Vong Sotheara and Nhep Sokha (RUPP), following Vickery and the 1996 Japanese/US/Khmer excavations, say there is
    **no city evidence at Ba Phnom**. **Angkor Borei** has a city occupied from before CE to the 7th c., and inscription
    K.222 "Vrai Krapās (Prey Kabbas) in Vyādhapura" places Vyādhapura in Takeo.
- Oc Eo (excavated by Louis Malleret, WWII era) yielded Roman, Indian and local goods. Some call it the first capital;
  Boisselier differed.
- Chinese sources:
  - The *Sanguo zhi* (Chen Shou, completed 289) records Funan embassies to Lü Dai, governor of Wu: c. 225–230 and 243.
  - The *Liang shu* (finished 636) preserves the reports of Wu envoys **Kang Tai (康泰)** and **Zhu Ying (朱應)**.
- Hun Tian legend (Liang shu):
  - Hun Tian (混塡) came from Jiao (徼) "in the south" after dreaming of a divine bow.
  - Queen **Liu Ye (柳葉, "willow leaf")** tried to seize his ship. He shot an arrow through her boat. She surrendered and
    married him, and he clothed her.
  - His son ruled seven towns.
  - The *Jin shu* gives the names Hun Hui / Ye Liu.
- Kaundinya:
  - Scholars tried to merge Hun Tian with **Kaundinya + nāgī Somā**, which first appears in the **Mỹ Sơn inscription C.96
    (658 CE)**: Kaundinya planted a spear received from Aśvatthāman and married Somā, daughter of the nāga king.
  - Vickery rejects the identification. "Hun Tian" has two syllables and is not "Kaundinya", and the Chinese story
    has no nāga and no brahmin.
- Later Kaundinya (Liang shu): a brahmin **Qiaochenru (僑陳如)** heard a voice telling him to rule Funan. He came via
  Panpan, was chosen king and "changed the laws to follow India". He succeeded **Chandan (天竺旃檀)**, who sent tame
  elephants in **357**.
- Dynasty and kings (Chinese names):
  - Hun Panhuang died aged 90.
  - His son Pan Pan reigned 3 years.
  - General **Fan Shiman (范師蔓)** was elected king. He built big ships, conquered 10+ states and called himself "great
    king of Funan". The article identifies him with Śrī Māra of the Võ Cạnh inscription, which it presents as Funanese
    per Finot 1927. He died campaigning against Jinlin ("golden frontier", Suvarṇabhūmi?).
  - His nephew **Fan Zhan (范旃)** usurped, and the article equates him with "Dharanindravarman". Fan Zhan sent Su Wu to
    India (the Murunda king) and **the 243 embassy (music and products)**.
  - Fan Chang (Shiman's son) killed Zhan and was in turn killed by general **Fan Xun (范尋)**. The article calls Fan Xun
    "អស្សាជយ". Fan Xun received Kang Tai and Zhu Ying c. 245–250 and sent embassies 268–287.
- 5th–6th c.:
  - Śrīndravarman sent tribute in 434–438.
  - **Kaundinya Jayavarman** sent the Indian monk **Nāgasena** to China in **484**, asking for help against Linyi
    (refused). He sent a coral Buddha in 503 and died in **514**.
  - His queen **Kulaprabhāvatī** and son **Guṇavarman** left Vaishnava inscriptions (Takeo; Prasat Pram Loveng / Tháp Mười
    K.5: "moon line of the Kaundinya lineage", "land won from the mud").
  - **Rudravarman**, son of a concubine, killed Guṇavarman. He was the last known king, reigning 514–c.545, and sent
    embassies 517–539. In 539 he told the Liang court of a 12-foot Buddha hair relic, and the Liang emperor sent a
    monk to fetch it. The Ta Prohm (Bati) inscription mentions him.
  - The Funanese monks **Saṅghapāla (សង្ឃបាល)** and **Mandrasena (មន្ទ្រសេន)** translated sutras in China (e.g. the
    *Saptaśatikā Prajñāpāramitā*).
- Society, according to Chinese sources:
  - Houses on stilts, rice ("sow once, harvest three times"), taxes in gold, silver, pearls and perfumes, slavery.
  - Ordeals: boiling water, red-hot chain, rice-ball test, crocodiles.
  - Four funeral modes. Books and archives existed.
  - Ships 30 m long with fish-shaped prow and stern. A Funan music troupe went to China and an "institute near Nanjing"
    was founded.
- Religion: Shaivism (Mount Motan, the Maheśvara cult). Vishnu at Phnom Da. Buddhism prominent in the 5th–6th c.
- Decline: Chenla, a vassal, absorbed Funan in the 6th–7th c. The article calls it "the first Khmer kingdom" but also
  quotes Vickery that with current evidence one **cannot** prove Funan's rulers were *not* Khmer.

### 02 – Oc Eo
- Archaeological site in Thoại Sơn district, An Giang (Vietnam). Excavated from 10 Feb 1944 by Malleret (the text says
  1942; the correct year is 1944). Area 450 ha.
- Canals link it to the sea and to Angkor Borei. The text gives 42 *miles*, which is wrong (it is ~70–90 km). A 1958
  aerial survey showed an ancient canal to Takeo.
- Finds: pottery, jewellery, moulds, coins, religious images, gold pieces imitating **Roman coins of the Antonine
  period**, and silver coins with a crested-argus bird.
- The Vietnamese historian Hà Văn Tấn questions whether Oc Eo should be called "Funan". The site may be Ptolemy's
  **Kattigara**. The Columbus and Ptolemy tangent is irrelevant to the film.

### 04 – History of Angkor Borei district
- Funan had three key sites: Oc Eo, Ba Phnom and Angkor Borei. The page says Angkor Borei is the oldest.
- Inscription K.549 (late Angkorian, 12th c.) is said to name the city Naravaranagara.
- Aerial photos show a brick wall ~6 km around, 6–8 m high. Glass and stone beads and burials date from ~400 BCE.
- "At least 10 temples from the 1st c. CE". Only Ashram Maha Rosei and Phnom Da survive.
- Asserts Funan stretched to the Malay peninsula and "perhaps Sumatra". Concludes "the Khmer capital in the Funan
  period really was at Angkor Borei … birthplace of Khmer civilisation."

### 05 – Chenla
- 真臘 Zhenla is the Chinese name for Cambodia after Funan.
- The first known princes are attested in inscriptions:
  - Vīravarman, father of Bhavavarman's sister, who married the brahmin Somaśarman (K.359).
  - **Chitrasena = Mahendravarman** (K.363). K.978 makes Bhavavarman a grandson of the cakravartin (Rudravarman?).
- **Īśānavarman** absorbed old Funan (*New Tang History*). His embassy reached China in 616–617. His inscriptions are
  dated 627 and 637 (Sambor Prei Kuk / Isanapura).
- Later rulers: Bhavavarman II (644, 655), Jayavarman I (657–c.690). Split into "Land" and "Water" Chenla c. 705–707.

### 06 / 07 / 08 / 12 – Kings and queen
- "Kaundinya I = Preah Thong = Kumērāj = Hun Tian". Reign "52 CE–end of 1st c." Called "an exiled king from Java".
  Cites the Mỹ Sơn 658 inscription (spear from Aśvatthāman, married the nāga king's daughter), "which seems to match
  the Preah Thong–Neang Neak story".
- "Soma = Liu Ye = Neang Neak = Dharavati", called "first female ruler of Cambodia", reigning to 52 CE. The name is
  explained as "child of the moon". The page uses both ព.ស. ៦៥៨ and គ.ស. ៦៥៨ for the same inscription (it is CE).
- Kaundinya II: tribute in 357 (wrong: 357 belongs to Chandan). Says the Kushans fled Samudragupta and "brought
  Kaundinya II".
- Kaundinya Jayavarman: reign 470–514. Chinese title "General of the South Pacifying …". Monks Nāgasena (484) and
  Bodhibhadra (509). Coral Buddha and ivory stupa sent in 503.

### 09 – Phnom Da
- Located in Angkor Borei district, Takeo. The lead says it was built in the 6th c. by **Rudravarman**. The history
  section says the present building is **12th c.** (or 11th c., restored under Suryavarman I) on 6th–7th c. foundations.
- The 8-armed Vishnu (2.7 m) and other Phnom Da-style statues are in the National Museum. On the UNESCO Tentative List
  since 1992 (resubmitted 2020).

### 11 / 14 / 15 – Preah Thong – Neang Neak legend (see §4)

### 13 – RFA Khmer, "Khmer civilisation in the Nokor Phnom era (part 5)"
- Repeats the Chinese ethnography: walled villages with moats and crocodiles, stilt houses, dark skin and curly hair,
  ordeals, no prisons, crocodile trial.
- Kingship was Indian-style. The king's title was "chakravartin" or **"kurung vnam"**, ruling vassals including
  Chenla. Taxes were paid in gold, silver, pearls and perfume.
- Says the society was **matriarchal: "women asked men in marriage."**
- Oc Eo was the port south of Ba The. It is said to receive "large ships carrying goods from the **Roman Empire** at
  the Red Sea". Finds include a Sanskrit seal and a **gold medallion dated 152 with the image of Antoninus**.
- Rice "sown once, harvested three times". 30 m ships.
- Says Khmer was the common language and Sanskrit the official one, and that Mahayana Buddhism mixed with **neak ta**
  belief was general.
- Royal cremation 7 days after death with ashes in a gold urn thrown in the river. Brick temples. Phnom Da sculptures
  (Vishnu, Balarama, Kalkin, Krishna Govardhana).

## 2. Claims-check table

| Claim (source) | Verdict | Reason |
|---|---|---|
| "Funan" (扶南) is a Chinese rendering of OK *bnaṃ/vnaṃ* "mountain" (01, 04, 13) | **Plausible, unproven** | Coedès's hypothesis, still widely repeated. Vickery and others doubt it, and the native name is unknown. Narrate it as "scholars have proposed…". |
| Embassies to Wu c. 225–230 and 243, recorded in *Sanguo zhi* (01) | **Supported** | Standard. The 243 embassy of Fan Zhan brought "musicians and local products". |
| Kang Tai and Zhu Ying visited Funan mid-3rd c. under Fan Xun (01, 06) | **Supported** | Their reports survive only in later quotations (*Liang shu*, encyclopaedias). |
| Funan music "institute near Nanjing" in 263 (01) | **Questionable** | Traced to an unsourced en.wiki line. The 243 tribute of musicians is attested, the institute is not. Drop it. |
| Hun Tian / Liu Ye story (01, 07) | **Supported as a Chinese legend** | It is in the *Liang shu* and *Jin shu*. It is a founding myth, not a datable event. |
| Hun Tian = Kaundinya = Preah Thong; Liu Ye = Soma = Neang Neak (06, 07) | **Questionable / later conflation** | The Kaundinya + Somā story first appears in a **658 CE** Cham inscription (Mỹ Sơn C.96). Vickery and Jacques reject Hun Tian = Kaundinya. The Preah Thong tale is a much later chronicle tradition. Present them as three parallel traditions. |
| Kaundinya I reigned "from 52 CE"; Soma "reigned until 52 CE" (06, 07) | **Unsupported** | No source gives these years. They are modern guesses presented as fact. |
| Hun Tian came "from Java" (06) | **Questionable** | 徼 (jiao) means "frontier / southern lands". India, the Malay peninsula and Indonesia are all proposed. |
| Fan Shiman = Śrī Māra of Võ Cạnh inscription (01) | **Questionable** | Coedès's old suggestion. Many scholars now treat Võ Cạnh as early Champa/Linyi and doubt the match. |
| Fan Zhan = "Dharanindravarman", Fan Xun = "Assajaya" (01, wiki) | **Unsupported** | Sanskrit names reconstructed by Khmer textbooks with no inscriptional basis. Use the Chinese names. |
| 357 tribute of tame elephants by "Chandan, an Indian" (01) | **Supported** | *Jin shu* and *Liang shu*. The Kushan / Scythian origin is Lévi's speculation. |
| 357 tribute was Kaundinya II's (08) | **Wrong** | That was Chandan. Kaundinya II comes after him (early 5th c.). |
| Kaundinya II, a brahmin "told by a voice", came via Panpan and Indianised the laws (01, 08) | **Supported as Chinese record** | *Liang shu*. The details are legendary. |
| Kaundinya Jayavarman: Nāgasena 484, coral Buddha 503, died 514 (01, 12) | **Supported** | *Nan Qi shu* and *Liang shu*. Standard chronology. |
| Rudravarman killed Guṇavarman and was the last king; embassies to 539; hair relic (01) | **Supported** | *Liang shu*. Calling him the "last king" is the standard view. |
| Saṅghapāla and Mandrasena translated sutras in Liang China (01, 04) | **Supported** | Buddhist catalogues. Early 6th c. |
| Funan was a single empire; Fan Shiman conquered 10+ states and ruled 5–6000 li (01) | **Questionable as phrased** | This is Chinese hyperbole. Modern view: a network of polities or city-states, sometimes unified. |
| Angkor Borei occupied from ~400 BCE; brick wall ~6 km (04) | **Supported** | LOMAP (Stark et al.) excavations show occupation from the mid-1st millennium BCE. The wall is ~6 km. |
| Angkor Borei = Vyādhapura = "the" Funan capital (01, 04) | **Plausible, debated** | Vickery, Vong Sotheara and the LOMAP evidence favour it. Ba Phnom is the older Coedès hypothesis. Say "probably one of the main centres". |
| Angkor Borei "at least 10 temples from the 1st c. CE" (04) | **Questionable** | Brick structures exist, but 1st-c. temples are not demonstrated. |
| Oc Eo first excavated by Malleret (02: 1942) | **Supported (date: 1944)** | Excavations began Feb 1944. |
| Gold medallion of **Antoninus Pius dated 152 CE** at Oc Eo (02, 13) | **Supported** | Malleret found Roman gold medallions (Antoninus Pius AD 152, Marcus Aurelius). Their arrival was probably indirect. |
| "Roman ships came to Oc Eo from the Red Sea" (13) | **Questionable** | Roman objects came via Indian and Southeast Asian intermediaries. There is no evidence of Roman ships there. |
| Oc Eo = Ptolemy's Kattigara (02) | **Plausible, unproven** | Proposed by some scholars and much debated. |
| Canal linking Oc Eo and Angkor Borei (02, 01) | **Supported** | Aerial survey and LOMAP. Canal ~70–90 km. The "42 miles" in 02 is a translation error. |
| Phnom Da temple built by Rudravarman in the 6th c. (09) | **Questionable** | The Phnom Da-style statues are late 6th–early 7th c. The standing tower is rebuilt and later. Ashram Maha Rosei is 7th c. |
| Funan society was **matriarchal; "women asked men in marriage"** (01, 13) | **Questionable** | This is an inference from the Liu Ye story and chronicle tropes. Chinese sources do not say Funan was matriarchal. |
| Mahayana Buddhism mixed with neak ta was the general religion (13) | **Questionable** | Projects later Cambodian religion back in time. The evidence shows Shaiva, Vaishnava and Buddhist cults. |
| Khmer the everyday language, Sanskrit official (13, 01) | **Plausible** | Inscriptions in Funan territory are in Sanskrit. Khmer is inferred from names and later inscriptions. |

| Preah Thong–Neang Neak wedding 267 BCE; Preah Thong born 312 BCE (11, 14, 15) | **Legendary** | These dates come from the Buddhist-era chronicles and are not historical. Never narrate them as dates. |
| Preah Thong's ~10 million followers; borders to Sukhothai/Phitsanulok (11, 14) | **Unsupported** | Chronicle hyperbole and anachronism (Sukhothai is 13th c.). |
| South Asian ancestry in an Angkor Borei individual (search result) | **Supported (nuanced)** | Changmai et al. 2022: ~40–50% in *one* 1st–2nd c. CE individual. Not population-wide. |

## 4. The Khmer telling of the Preah Thong – Neang Neak legend

**Sources:** 11, 14, 15. They follow Ros Chantrabot's "ប្រវត្តិសាស្ត្រខ្មែរ ភាគរឿងនិទាន" and the royal chronicles. 14 also
includes the Buddha prophecy from the *Prah Khan / Buddhist chronicle* tradition.

### Summary of the chronicle version
1. **Buddha's prophecy (ពុទ្ធទំនាយ).**
   - The Buddha, aged 80, rests with Ānanda under a **ធ្លក** tree (*Fagraea*) on an island called **គោកធ្លក**, above
     the burrow of the nāga king.
   - A **monitor lizard (ត្រកួត)** licks its lips with its forked tongue. The Buddha smiles and prophesies that this
     island will become a great kingdom. The lizard will be reborn as a prince who comes to rule it, and the nāga king
     will create land for him.
   - The tail of the story is pejorative: the "forked tongue" means the people will be two-faced. **Do not use that
     part.**
2. **Exile.**
   - Preah Thong is the 4th son of King Ādityavaṃśa of **ឥន្ទបត្ថ / ឥន្ទព័ទ្ធ** (Indraprastha). He fails to bow to
     his youngest brother, who is acting as regent, and is banished.
   - He sails or marches south with followers and lands at Kok Thlok near the Dangrek range. He defeats a Cham king
     and founds **នគរគោកធ្លក**.
3. **Meeting.**
   - Resting under the thlok tree, he is trapped by the rising tide.
   - At night **ព្រះនាងធារាវត្តី**, the nāga king's daughter, comes up to play. They fall in love.
4. **Wedding.**
   - Seven days later he brings gifts. The nāga king accepts him as son-in-law, **drinks up the sea to create dry land**
     as a wedding gift and builds palaces.
   - On returning to the nāga realm, Preah Thong **holds the sash of Neang Neak (តោងស្បៃនាងនាគ)** and follows her
     down. This is the origin of the Khmer wedding rite of the groom holding the bride's sbai.
   - The chronicle gives the date 11th waxing day of Visakha, B.E. 277 = 267 BCE. This is legendary.
5. **Aftermath (chronicle only).**
   - War with the Cham king អស្សជ័យរាជ, helped by nāga troops. His five sons are appointed governors.
   - Borders are said to reach Sukhothai. These are anachronistic. Skip them.

### Key Khmer phrases (natural, quotable)
- «ព្រះថោង នាងនាគ» / «រឿងព្រេងព្រះថោងនាងនាគ»
- «ដើមកំណើតនៃការកកើតទឹកដីខ្មែរ» ("the origin of the birth of the Khmer land")
- «ព្រះថោងតោងស្បៃនាងនាគ» ("Preah Thong holds Neang Neak's sash"). Used for the wedding rite and the 2022 Sihanoukville statue.
- «ស្ដេចនាគបឺតទឹកសមុទ្រ បង្កើតជាដីគោក» ("the nāga king drank up the sea, creating dry land")
- «កោះគោកធ្លក» / «នគរគោកធ្លក» / «ដើមធ្លក»
- «ជាវាសនាដែលគូគាប់បានជួបគ្នា» ("it was destiny that the couple met", 15)
- Wedding-song tradition: «ប្ដីតោងស្បៃប្រពន្ធ»

### How Khmer texts relate it to Kaundinya and Soma
- km.wiki (06, 07) **equates them outright**: «កៅណ្ឌិន្យទី១ ឬ ព្រះថោង ឬ កុម៉ែរាជ ឬ ហ៊ុនទៀន» and «សោមា ឬ លីវយី ឬ
  នាងនាគ ឬ ធារាវត្តី».
- The pages cite the Mỹ Sơn 658 inscription ("received a spear from the brahmin Aśvatthāman, threw it to fix the site of
  the city, married Somā daughter of the nāga king") and add: «បើមើលតាមរឿង វាហាក់ដូចជាស៊ីគ្នានឹងរឿងព្រះថោង នាងនាគ»
  ("it seems to match the Preah Thong–Neang Neak story").
- 15 (Kampuchea Thmey) keeps it as **រឿងព្រេង** and does not equate it with Kaundinya.
- The Funan main article (01) gives Vickery's objections.

**Recommended framing for the film:** present three layers.
1. The **Chinese account** (3rd c. envoys): Hun Tian and Liu Ye.
2. The **inscription** (Cham, 658 CE): Kaundinya and the nāgī Somā.
3. The **Khmer folk and chronicle legend** (much later): Preah Thong and Neang Neak.

Khmer tradition links all three. Historians debate whether they describe the same people.

Suggested line: «ប្រវត្តិវិទូខ្លះភ្ជាប់រឿងនេះទៅនឹងកៅណ្ឌិន្យ និងនាងសោមា ប៉ុន្តែជារឿងព្រេង មិនមែនជាព្រឹត្តិការណ៍ដែលអាចកំណត់កាលបរិច្ឆេទបានទេ។»
